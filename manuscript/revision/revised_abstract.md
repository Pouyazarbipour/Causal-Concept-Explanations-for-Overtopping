# Abstract

Each test in the CLASH wave-overtopping database carries a Reliability Factor (RF) and a Complexity Factor (CF), both ordinal from 1 to 4. These ratings are used to weight or exclude tests when data-driven predictors are developed, but their validity for that decision has not been tested. We audited all 10 532 tests: 9 333 positive-discharge tests enter the regression and 10 389 enter an occurrence (wet/dry) model. The ratings were evaluated against out-of-fold residuals of nested, geometry-grouped cross-validated learners. Tests with RF = 4 show 2.34 [1.41, 3.81] times the residual variance of RF = 1 tests. The result holds for every learner and validation scheme, but RF 1–3 show no ordered trend and near-replicate repeatability does not rise systematically with RF. CF shows no ordered relation to geometry-level error. A nested decomposition attributes 58 % of the residual variance to within-geometry variation, 29 % to geometries and 13 % to test series. Near-replicate variance accounts for 56 % [31 %, 93 %] of the within-geometry part, so that part is not pure measurement error. On identical held-out tests, excluding or zero-weighting category-4 tests changes the error of the remaining tests by at most 0.007 log10 units. With a learner re-tuned for each policy the retained tests gain at most 0.014 (not significant). Both ways, the error of RF = 4 tests increases by +0.27 to +0.31. Retaining all tests and scaling group-weighted split-conformal intervals by RF, CF and the physical inputs raises RF = 4 coverage of 90 % intervals from 77 % to 89 % and gives the best interval score. CF = 3 tests remain under-covered (82 %). Exclusion also changes engineering decisions: it raises the share of missed exceedances of allowable discharges for RF = 4 tests and removes almost all depth-limited tests. The ratings should be evaluated empirically before they are used to discard data, and are better used to manage predictive uncertainty than to exclude tests.
